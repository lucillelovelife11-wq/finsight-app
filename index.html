How the three statements are built

Illustrative structure, not a company-specific filing. Actual captions and presentation vary by industry and GAAP reporting choices.

INCOME STATEMENT · PERFORMANCE

From Revenue to Net Income

Revenue / Net sales Top line
− Cost of goods sold (COGS)
= Gross profit
− Operating expenses (SG&A, R&D, etc.)
= Operating income (EBIT)*
− Net interest and other non-operating items
= Pretax income
− Income tax expense
= Net income (NI)

*EBIT is an analytical proxy; classification of non-operating items and depreciation varies by issuer. Net income flows to retained earnings and starts the indirect CFO reconciliation.

BALANCE SHEET · FINANCIAL POSITION

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets
Current: cash, receivables, inventory, prepaids
Noncurrent: PPE net, intangibles, investments
= Total assets
Liabilities
Current: payables, accrued expenses, deferred revenue
Noncurrent: debt, lease obligations, other liabilities
Equity
Contributed capital + retained earnings − treasury stock
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Net income increases retained earnings, less dividends and other equity movements. Closing cash must reconcile to the cash flow statement.

CASH FLOW STATEMENT · CASH MOVEMENT

From Net Income to Ending Cash

Net income (indirect method)
+ Noncash charges (e.g., depreciation)
± Changes in operating working capital
= Cash from operations (CFO)
± Investing cash flows (CFI): PPE, investments
± Financing cash flows (CFF): debt, equity, dividends
= Net change in cash*
+ Beginning cash
= Ending cash → Balance Sheet

*Include foreign-exchange effects when applicable and use a consistent cash / restricted-cash definition.

Cross-statement linkages

Income statement Net income→ Balance sheet Retained earnings
Income statement Net income→ Cash flow Indirect CFO starting point
Cash flow Ending cash→ Balance sheet Cash & equivalents
Balance sheet Receivables ↑→ Cash flow CFO adjustment ↓
PPE acquisition CFI outflow→ Balance sheet PPE ↑
Depreciation Expense ↑→ BS PPE net ↓ & CFS noncash add-back

ROE & Three-Factor DuPont Analysis

ROE = Return on Sales × Total Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage. Use average balance-sheet denominators when available.

Financial inputs

Calculated drivers

ROE = (NI / Sales) × (Sales / Avg. Assets) × (Avg. Assets / Avg. Equity)

NI and equity should be attributable to the same shareholder group. Negative or zero average equity makes standard ROE interpretation problematic.

Operating ROA & Financial Leverage

Separates operating performance from financing and non-operating investments.

Operating inputs

Operating performance

NOPAT = NI + (Interest expense − Interest revenue) × (1 − tax rate)
NOA = Total assets − Operating liabilities − Non-operating assets
OpROA = NOPAT / NOA

This calculator uses a simplified after-tax net interest adjustment. The course also emphasizes classification judgments and the need to match flow with invested operating capital. For a period analysis, replace the single balance with average NOA where appropriate.

Accruals & Cash Flow Quality

The course defines accruals as Net Income − Operating Cash Flow. Differences are diagnostic, not proof of manipulation.

Income-to-cash bridge

Diagnostics

Accruals = NI − OCF
FCF (simple) = OCF − CapEx

The simplified receivables change is not a substitute for credit-sales turnover or allowance analysis. Compare across periods and consider business model, non-cash compensation, working capital, and one-time items.

Discounted Cash Flow · Perpetuity Growth

Illustrative enterprise-value DCF using projected free cash flow to the firm (FCFF), discount rate and terminal growth.

Assumptions

DCF outputs

Terminal Value₅ = FCFF₆ / (WACC − g)
Enterprise Value = PV(explicit FCFF) + PV(terminal value)

Assumes constant annual growth for five years and a stable perpetual terminal rate. Equity value = enterprise value − net debt; other claims and non-operating assets may require adjustments in real valuations.
